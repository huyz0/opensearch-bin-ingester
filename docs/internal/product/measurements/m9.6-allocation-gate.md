# M9.6 allocation gate

Status: measured on 2026-09-21. This is NFR-6's leading indicator, not proof
of memory flatness; the memory-flatness soak remains the proof obligation.

The named gate contains four logical JMH benchmarks, selected by the M9.5 JFR
profile: the AWS SDK chunked-body decoder, `ConcurrentHashMap` table
initialisation, `HashMap` resize pressure, and B3 compression at 256 KiB with
zstd level 3. The full zstd/lz4/none matrix and block-size comparison belongs
to M9.7. The gate runs one fork, `-prof gc`, two one-second measurement
iterations, and stays within the L1 budget. The manual suite was also run with
two forks, five one-second warmups, five one-second measurement iterations and
`-prof gc`.

## Noise-floor derivation

The noise run used ten independent JMH forks per benchmark, one 100 ms warmup
and one 500 ms measurement iteration, with `-prof gc`. Variance is the sample
standard deviation of the ten `gc.alloc.rate.norm` samples divided by their
mean, expressed as a percentage. The threshold is `min(25%, max(5%, 3 ×
variance))`. All four benchmarks were below the cap, so none was dropped.

| Benchmark | Baseline B/op | Samples | Variance | Threshold |
|---|---:|---:|---:|---:|
| AWS chunked body | 292,063.762 | 10 | 0.003118% | 5% |
| B3 zstd level 3, 256 KiB | 263,344.858 | 10 | 0.000031% | 5% |
| `ConcurrentHashMap` init | 448.001 | 10 | 0.000012% | 5% |
| `HashMap` resize | 34,896.057 | 10 | 0.000005% | 5% |

The committed machine-readable baseline is
[`allocation-baseline.json`](../../../../bench/src/jmh/resources/allocation-baseline.json).
The gate reads only JMH's `secondaryMetrics["gc.alloc.rate.norm"]`; throughput
is deliberately not a gate.
